Octopuses change color in their sleep

Researchers have found that octopuses change color even while asleep. In a study of four animals, skin patterns during quiet sleep differed from those during active sleep, a phase that lasts about a minute.

The finding adds to a growing list of ways that cephalopod sleep resembles our own. Earlier work showed that octopuses deprived of rest later sleep longer, a hallmark of sleep in mammals and birds.

The team suggests this active phase may resemble dreaming, replaying patterns the animals used while awake. "It looks like they are re-running the day," said the lead author.

Not everyone is convinced. Some biologists caution that the sample was small and that color changes could be simple muscle twitches rather than anything like a dream.
